## 0. Summary

1. **At the same ε per round, pure-DP Laplace noise costs far less list quality than the Gaussian noise used since doc 16 (development, w1).** Share of the plain-sampling selector value lost (mean price ÷ mean plain value over logs; central model, nominal per-round accounting; Gaussian = doc 18's unit-length reports with analytic Gaussian noise, δ = 10⁻⁵; Laplace = every person's vector scaled to L1 norm 1, scale 1/ε, δ = 0):

   | People (logs) | plain value | ε = 2 | ε = 4 | ε = 8 |
   |---|---:|---|---|---|
   | 100 (27) | 0.599 | 78% → 63% | 66% → **41%** | 50% → 18% |
   | 300 (24) | 0.689 | 60% → 36% | 42% → **15%** | 22% → 6% |
   | 1,000 (17) | 0.752 | 30% → 9% | 13% → **4%** | 5% → 2% |

   Paired selector-value gain of Laplace over Gaussian (95% interval over logs, seed 7): at ε = 4, +0.151 (+0.115 to +0.183; 26 of 27 logs) at 100 people, +0.184 (+0.134 to +0.231; 23 of 24) at 300, +0.067 (+0.041 to +0.094; 14 of 17) at 1,000. The rough expectation written before the run (Laplace at ε behaves like Gaussian at 2ε, from the expected maximum noise over 10⁵ items) was a loss at ε = 4 about equal to the Gaussian's at ε = 8 (50% at 100 people here); the Laplace loses 41%.
2. **The gain is the noise, not the new normalisation, and it has exceptions.** Swapping only the noise on the same one-row signal (`lap c=1` minus `gauss c=1`) is positive in every log at ε = 4 at every tested size (27/27, 24/24, 17/17; +0.190, +0.228, +0.091). The L1 unit (each person total weight 1) is about as good as a one-row cap under Laplace noise (differences between −0.003 and +0.008; it wins in 17 of 17 logs at 1,000 people at ε = 4 and 8, by at most 0.004 on average), and it loses signal where people have many rows: at ε = 4 ml-latest is 0.143 (100 people) and 0.130 (300) worse than the Gaussian unit length, and at 1,000 people the three Goodreads logs are 0.005 to 0.020 worse (Amazon logs +0.089, the other four logs −0.004). The noise-free cost of the L1 unit is 5.2%, 3.8%, 1.9% of the plain value (Gaussian unit length 1.2%, 0.9%, 0.7%).
3. **Per-round ε is not equal privacy for someone who takes part many times.** Pure ε-DP adds up linearly over rounds, Gaussian noise between the square root and linear (lifetime ε at δ = 10⁻⁵ for a per-round Gaussian ε = 4: 10.4, 16.1, 49.9 after 5, 10, 52 participations, worst case over the rows a person sends). At equal lifetime ε the Laplace noise has the smaller standard deviation up to k = 11, 18, 52 participations and the smaller expected maximum up to k = 2, 3, 4 (Gaussian ε = 2, 4, 8; the first k where Laplace is no longer smaller is 12, 19, 53 and 3, 4, 5; at ε = 8 both crossings are near ties); at ε = 4 the standard deviation is 3.1×, 1.6×, 1.2× smaller at k = 1, 5, 10 and the Gaussian ÷ Laplace expected maximum is 1.62, 0.84, 0.39 at k = 1, 5, 52. The simulation grid brackets k = 5: Laplace at ε = 2 and 4 (just below the matched 2.08 and 4.43) against Gaussian at ε = 4 and 8 stays ahead on selector value (+0.018, +0.042, +0.025 for ε 2 against 4 and +0.057, +0.048, +0.007 for ε 4 against 8, at 100, 300, 1,000 people; two of the six intervals include 0), although the expected-maximum yardstick already favours Gaussian there, so that yardstick does not predict the outcome here. The bracket compares per-round noise levels matched to a lifetime of 5 participations; it is not a simulation of a design that enforces a cap of 5. In the simulated draws (draw 0, 52 of about 147 steady weeks) the typical drawn person is drawn once (twice or more: 1.7%, 4.5%, 9.7% of random reporters at 100, 300, 1,000 people a week; five times or more: about 0%, 0%, 0.1% in the median log), but in the logs with the most repeats one person is drawn 11, 23, 17 times (random reporters) and 30, 47, 43 times (heavy volunteers). Without a cap a guarantee has to cover every round; nothing beyond the k = 5 bracket was simulated.
4. **Bad rounds are mostly shared by every draw and show up in the full-community list too (weekly cells at 100 and 300 people, months-long cells at 300 people; not at 30 people with months-long rounds).** The draw-to-draw noise is 7.4% to 12.5% of a draw's variance over rounds, the mean over draws of the sampled list's per-round quality correlates 0.86 to 0.94 with that of the full-community list (no sampling at all), and the full list's bad-round gap (mean minus 10th percentile, 0.086 to 0.159) is about as large as or larger than the sampled list's (0.088 to 0.121; doc 12's 0.121 at 300 people is reproduced). At 30 people with months-long rounds the draws dominate (46% of the variance, correlation 0.25) and doc 12's gap there (0.089) is not explained here.
5. **Full-list quality is persistent from round to round, and the change between rounds goes with it.** A bottom-decile round of the full list is followed by another one 47% (weekly, 100 people), 46% (300) and 33% (months-long, defined in 17 of 24 logs, which have two or three such rounds, so this is coarse) of the time, against 10% under independence, and its quality correlates +0.66 to +0.67 (Spearman) with the previous round's in every log. Slow movement of the level and the overlap of the list with its own past through its memory can produce this (not separated here); after first differencing the autocorrelation is negative weekly (−0.31 and −0.30) and near zero months-long (−0.10), which fits a slowly moving level plus round-to-round noise weekly but does not tell a level from a trend. It correlates −0.42, −0.45 (weekly) and −0.66 (months-long) with the change between the two rounds before the scoring round, a change that shares no round with the change into it (it lies inside the list's own memory, so it can also degrade the list directly). The change into the scoring round explains about half of the variation weekly and four fifths months-long (adjusted R² 0.51 and 0.82, together with thin-round and newborn measures) but nearly restates the score. Thin scoring rounds and newborn items matter little (standardised coefficients +0.03 to +0.17 and −0.09 to −0.13). The scoring round is not noisy in the median log (split-half reliability 0.92 to 0.99) but is in five small weekly logs (0.71 to 0.78).
6. **For the agenda:** the premise of "ask more people when a round is unstable" is not supported here and not refuted. The sampling gap (full list minus sampled list: mean 0.10 at 100 people, 0.05 at 300, weekly) persists from round to round (Spearman +0.67, +0.52) but is not explained by the preceding change (−0.05, +0.01 against the lag-2 change), and at months-long rounds it is smaller after big changes (−0.66 to −0.70); whether a signal the server itself can see tracks the gap is untested (doc 12). The Laplace line moves to a registered test (section 5).

What the literature check said, in one paragraph. For a large self-selected sample plus a small random one, propensity, calibration, imputation and doubly robust methods all need covariates that explain both who volunteers and what they pick (here only activity), and none sees a taste bias (Yang, Kim and Song arXiv:1903.05212; Kim, Park, Chen and Wu arXiv:1812.10694); prediction-powered inference needs the large sample to come from the target population (Angelopoulos et al. arXiv:2301.09633); the first read-only agent judged that only a shrinkage or test-and-pool rule with a data-chosen weight uses the audit's own counts (Rosenman et al. arXiv:2002.06708: needs dimension at least four, risk bounded as the bias grows; Gao and Yang 2024: if the test rejects, keep only the probability data). For private top-k with 100–1,000 users the second agent found no published mechanism that beats Gaussian noise on every item (its reading: stability histograms and thresholding serve an unknown item set, ours is public; sample-and-threshold and TrieHH need 10⁴–10⁶ users: Bharadwaj and Cormode arXiv:2112.05693, Zhu et al. arXiv:1902.08534), found no published number for how many users a private top 10 needs, and found a private choice of the clip bound (Liu et al. arXiv:2206.03008, "almost a two-approximation" of the best bound in hindsight, shown for the clip-and-add-noise recipe). Details and the checks are in doc 90, section 6, item 6.

## 2. Programme L: what was done and what came out

1. **Samplers** (`scripts/ssp/lap_core.py`, patched onto the engine like `price_core.py`, nothing in `deep_core.py` or `price_core.py` edited). `lapn:eps=E,C=1`: the same people as the std sampler; every person's count vector (item → rows in the round) is scaled to L1 norm at most 1 (a person with r rows gives each row weight 1/r), summed, Laplace noise of scale 1/E on every item already in the catalogue; one person added or removed changes the sum by at most 1 in L1, so the idealised release is E-DP with δ = 0. `lap:eps=E,c=1`: one random row per person (the cap-one signal of doc 18's `gauss:eps,c=1`), the same Laplace noise. A non-finite or non-positive ε is rejected.
2. **Cells** (`scripts/ssp/tune_lap.py`). Built from the stored weekly cells of window w1 (7-day rounds): 100 people (stage a, 27 logs), 300 (stage d, 24 logs) and 1,000 (stage a, 17 logs), 68 cells, 8 draws each; every cell adds the days arms under 15 samplers: at ε = 2, 4, 8 the Gaussian unit length `gaussn`, the Laplace L1 unit `lapn`, the Gaussian one-row cap `gauss c=1` and the Laplace one-row cap `lap c=1`, and the three noise-free references at ε = 10⁶ (the Gaussian reference still has σ = 7·10⁻⁴ per item, the Laplace reference scale 10⁻⁶; both negligible against the weights). All arms in a cell see the same people. The plain-sampling arms reproduce the stored cell exactly (68 of 68, `nd100` and `selfnd`). Price = selector value of the plain arm minus the selector value under the sampler (selector and regret as in docs 14–16; `an_stress.rule_values`). Intervals: bootstrap over logs, seed 7, 4,000 resamples (`an_weekly.boot`). 47 + 15 minutes of compute for the first 51 and the last 17 cells.
3. **Checks** (`scripts/ssp/check_lap.py`; four logs, 40 rounds): the noise-free arms pick the same people as the std sampler (equal supports), the total weight equals the number of people, the added noise on items nobody rated is Laplace with scale 1/ε (mean 0, standard deviation within 0.3% of √2/ε, tail share within 2% of e⁻⁴), no noise outside the catalogue. An independent prober found the maximum L1 change from removing one person to be 1.0000001 to 1.0000005 (float32 rounding) on five logs; two of twelve Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests rejected Laplace at p ≈ 0.015, and 36 further tests (six draws, 60 rounds, about 5·10⁵ values each) gave a smallest p of 0.046, 2.8% below 0.05 and uniform p-values (p = 0.64), so those two were chance.
4. **Equal-lifetime arithmetic** (`tune_lap.lifetime_rows`; arithmetic, not a certified accounting; worst case over the rows a person sends). Pure ε-DP adds up (kε; Dwork and Roth Cor. 3.15, a fixed tuple of mechanisms). k independent Gaussian releases N(0, σ²) with per-round L2 sensitivity 1 are equivalent to one release with σ/√k (only the sum of the squared sensitivities matters), so the lifetime ε at δ = 10⁻⁵ is the ε whose analytic-Gaussian σ equals σ/√k; the Laplace per-round ε with the same lifetime ε is lifetime/k. Allowing δ = 10⁻⁵ for the Laplace release does not help at these ε (a zCDP route gives 33 against 10.4 at k = 5, 57 against 50 at k = 52). A first version used the zCDP conversion for the Gaussian side, which a reviewer showed to be 7–16% generous to Laplace; the expected maxima use exact constants (4.53 σ for 10⁵ Gaussian values, 12.09 b for Laplace), after a critic showed the asymptotic formulas favoured Laplace by about 10%.

## 3. Programme R: what makes a round hard

1. **Question and data.** Doc 12, section 4: the 10th-percentile round is 0.09 (30 people) to 0.12 (300 people) NDCG below the mean and the gap does not shrink with n; "drift between rounds, not sampling noise" was left untested (and the two n used different log sets, 28 and 24 logs). `scripts/ssp/an_bad_rounds.py` uses no new sampling: for each log and n it compares the stored per-round NDCG of the sampled list (best fixed memory in hindsight, weekly cells of w1: 100 people on 27 logs and 300 on 24; months-long plan cells of doc 12: `decay:h=2`, 24 logs having both 30 and 300 people) with the full-community list of the same memory (recomputed as `FullDecay` does: it matches the engine's `fulldecay` arm to 5·10⁻⁶ on steady weekly rounds; the deep cells are a draw-mean of a tie-randomised ceiling). Steady rounds only (from round 8); 145–147 rounds per weekly log, 19–21 per months-long log; the weekly memory is 2 to 64 rounds.

3. **Scoring-round reliability** (a reviewer's point): the full list is scored against the whole community's next round, which is itself a finite round. Scoring the same list against two random halves of the round's people and stepping the correlation up (Spearman–Brown) gives a median 0.93 weekly (quartiles 0.84 and 0.96, smallest 0.71 for amazon-musical-instruments; five of 27 logs below 0.8) and 0.99 months-long.
4. **Newborn items** are items whose first positive anywhere in the whole log falls in the scoring round (a first version counted items first seen in the 156-week window; 0.075 against 0.040 in the median log).

### Interpretation

1. **At 100 or more people (weekly) and at 300 people (months-long) the bad rounds are shared by every draw and show up in the full-community list**, so they are not the server's sampling luck; at the median the full list's gap is as large as or larger than the sampled list's (weekly, 300 people: 0.086 against 0.088, the other cells larger). The correlation uses the mean over draws, which smooths sampling noise; a single server's draw is expected to correlate less (not computed). At 30 people with months-long rounds the draws dominate (46% of the variance) and doc 12's gap there (0.089 against 0.121 at 300 people on the same 24 logs) is not explained: it is narrower although the noise is larger, and a floor effect is a candidate (the median list quality is 0.21 at 30 people against 0.46 at 300; untested). No weekly 30-people cell exists, so 30 people and months-long rounds cannot be told apart.
2. **Full-list quality is persistent from round to round.** A bottom-decile round is followed by another one 46–47% of the time weekly (10% under independence) and the quality correlates +0.66 to +0.67 with the previous round in every log. A slowly moving level gives this, and for long memories (16 to 64 rounds in 17 of 27 weekly logs at 100 people and 11 of 24 at 300; 2 to 4 rounds in five and eight logs) consecutive full lists are nearly the same list scored against consecutive community rounds, so part of the persistence may be built in (not measured). After first differencing the autocorrelation is negative weekly (−0.31, −0.30), which fits a moving level plus round-to-round noise but also scoring noise alone, and does not separate a level from a trend; months-long it is near zero (−0.10). The lag-2 change goes with the score (−0.42 to −0.45 weekly, −0.66 months-long) but lies inside the list's own memory and may stand in for the change into the scoring round; the autocorrelation of the change series and a partial correlation were not computed. The change into the scoring round explains more (adjusted R² 0.51 and 0.82) but is nearly a restatement of the score of a one-round list (Spearman −0.87 to −0.90 in a reviewer's three logs), so that R² is descriptive. The rest is not explained by anything measured here.
3. **Thin scoring rounds and newborn items are small effects** at the median; small communities carry real scoring noise (reliability 0.71 to 0.78 in five logs).
4. **Extra people for unstable rounds** (agenda item 3). Extra sample only helps where the sampling gap is large and can be foreseen. The gap is not constant (its spread is 0.27 to 0.46 of the full list's spread weekly), it persists from round to round (+0.52 to +0.67), and neither the change into the scoring round (−0.13, −0.05) nor the lag-2 change (−0.05, +0.01) explains it at weekly rounds; at months-long rounds it is smaller after big changes (−0.66 to −0.70), the opposite of the premise. The gap is the gain from an unlimited sample, not the marginal gain from a few more people. A signal the server itself sees, such as its own self-score within one log, could still track the gap; doc 12 says that was not tested and this analysis does not test it. The simple form of faster forgetting after a detected change (doc 11, `drift:z=1.5,f=0.5`) gave +0.0008 against `decay:h=2`.

## 4. Limits and disclosures

1. **Development only.** Window w1 is the development window (it is also the window where doc 14's selector was designed); the Laplace samplers were not part of any registry; the thresholds of a registered test are still to be written (section 5). The expectation "Laplace at ε ≈ Gaussian at 2ε" was written before the run from the expected maximum noise over 10⁵ items; the observed gains are larger than that rough rule at 100 and 300 people (Laplace at ε = 4 beats the Gaussian at ε = 8 there: 0.243 against 0.301 lost at 100 people), as the critic expected for long memories (at 100 people every private cell picks the longest memory), so the standard deviation matters as well as the maximum.
2. **Per-round ε, central model, nominal accounting** (as docs 16 and 18): add/remove neighbours, a trusted server, ε per round, no amplification by sampling. Gaussian arms have δ = 10⁻⁵ and Laplace arms δ = 0: per round that is conservative for Laplace (δ = 0 is the stronger guarantee), over many rounds it penalises Laplace (section 2, point 4). **Equal lifetime privacy was simulated only at the k = 5 bracket**, and no cap on participation was simulated; a differential-privacy guarantee is about the most exposed person, not the median one. The exposure counts come from one draw over 52 of about 147 steady weeks, and a round with at most n active people draws everybody (which is why the two beer logs dominate the largest counts); without a cap the guarantee has to cover every round.
3. **The catalogue.** Noise sits on every item already in the catalogue, taken as the set of items anyone in the community has rated up to and including that round (`first <= k`): it is data-dependent, so the E-DP statement holds only for the idealised mechanism under a public catalogue (as in docs 12, 16, 18), and it puts noise on fewer items than a truly public catalogue would, so absolute prices are optimistic. The contrasts are not independent of it: the Laplace maximum grows like ln N and the Gaussian like √(ln N), so a larger public catalogue shrinks the Laplace advantage (10⁵ → 10⁶ items: the ratio of expected maxima falls by about 7%). The samplers run in floating point; textbook floating-point Laplace noise is not exactly pure DP (Mironov 2012, not read here).
4. **The L1 unit changes the target as well as the noise**: it counts one vote per person while the truth is row-weighted, which is why it is 5.2% / 3.8% / 1.9% worse without noise and loses on many-row logs (ml-latest, Goodreads). The clean noise-only comparison is the one-row column. A looser L1 bound (C = 2, 4) was not run; the best bound may depend on the log, as in doc 18.
5. **Sizes not run:** 3,000 people (doc 18 has no confirmation cell there either), rounds other than 7 days, single-use participants, a window other than w1. **Statistics:** intervals over logs treat logs as independent (20 of 27 are Amazon); one seed and 8 draws per cell; the three sizes use different log sets (27, 24 and 17 logs; the 300-people cells come from stage d), so a change of the gain from 300 to 1,000 people mixes size with the log set.
6. **Bad-round numbers are descriptive.** Medians over logs treat logs as independent. The weekly cells use the hindsight best fixed memory; the full list is a ceiling for that memory, not for any memory. The months-long cells have 19–21 steady rounds per log, so the R² with three predictors is inflated (adjusted values are given). The deep cadence uses the plan cells of doc 12, whose draws use tie-randomised ceilings (differences up to 0.02 per draw, at most 0.0005 on the medians of the standardised coefficients, 0.016 on one log's correlation). Drift is measured between consecutive rounds of the full community's counts (which a 100-person server never sees) while the list's memory is longer (up to 64 rounds); a memory-weighted drift was not computed. The reliability uses one random split per round.
7. **Reviews are same-provider, not an outside audit.** Before the run: a design critic overturned several points in the draft agenda (the std-deviation yardstick, "new", the falsifier for bad rounds, the logic of extra people, a correlation read as taste, packing tests into w2) and a fact check confirmed the external quotes it covered (0 wrong; the user count of Liu et al.'s experiments, its venue and the symbol in TrieHH's threshold were not confirmed and are not used). After the run: an Opus reviewer rebuilt every Laplace number from the raw rows without the project code (all matched to 5·10⁻⁶), including the selector re-implemented from doc 14, and found the per-round caveat, the generous zCDP conversion, the missing ε checks and the weaker checks (all fixed); an Opus reviewer recomputed the bad-round table for three logs and both cadences (identical) and found the near-tautology of the change measure, the scoring-round noise and the newborn definition (all added); a prober (sensitivity within float rounding, the Laplace noise matches), a verifier (byte-identical reruns of every table; `check_pool.py` still 0 mismatches) and a formula check (Dwork and Roth Thm 3.6 / Cor. 3.15 for a fixed tuple of mechanisms, Bun and Steinke Prop. 1.3, 1.6, Lemma 2.3). Three critic passes on this document found no wrong number but readings that said more than the data support, and each ended FAIL on the sentences then in the text: the equal-lifetime paragraph, exposure measured only at 100 people, the lag-1 change sharing a round, extra people judged against a change not known in advance, the limits on the catalogue and on windows, the logic of section 5; in the second pass "runs" of hard rounds resting on an autocorrelation that a slow trend and the list's own memory also produce, the cap that an equal-lifetime comparison assumes, and a backwards sentence about pooling draws; in the third the sampler behind the "47 draws" count, the persistence wording and the labels of the crossovers. All are changed above, and the exposure and persistence columns were added for them; no fourth pass was run.
8. **Literature** was read by agents, not by me; unconfirmed details are listed in doc 90 (change log item 9 and section 6, item 6).
9. **Small logs.** The scoring round is noisy (reliability 0.71 to 0.78) in five weekly logs, four of them communities with under 1,500 active people a week (musical-instruments 614, industrial-and-scientific 635, ratebeer 1,250, baby-products 1,454; the fifth, arts-crafts-and-sewing, has 2,595).

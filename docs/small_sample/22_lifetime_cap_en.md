# 22. Does the Laplace advantage survive a lifetime guarantee? A cap on how often one person is drawn (development stage, not a test)

Chinese version: [`22_lifetime_cap_zh.md`](22_lifetime_cap_zh.md). Follows doc 21 (Leo, after the plain-language reading of doc 21 that said "about 3 times the people" per round, asked whether new runs were needed). **Status: development on window w1** (68 cells: 100 people on 27 logs, 300 on 24, 1,000 on 17; 156 weekly rounds per cell). Nothing is registered and nothing here is a verdict; the numbers decide what to register (section 5). Same-provider reviews only. Nothing was downloaded, installed or executed outside the project's own code, and no new literature was read.

## 0. Summary

1. **A cap on how often one person is drawn rarely binds in these logs, and its cost is small.** The cap counts every one of the 156 weekly rounds. In the median log the people per round are unchanged at every size (equal to plain sampling), and few people would be excluded anyway: in uncapped sampling 1.7%, 4.5% and 9.7% of the people drawn are drawn twice or more in 52 weeks (median log, 100 / 300 / 1,000 people; doc 21's exposure table; over the cap's 156 rounds the shares are larger). Over 36 noisy contrasts the selector value moves by −0.016 to +0.011; six of 36 intervals exclude 0, all above (1.8 expected in either direction if every contrast were null, 0.9 above; two only barely; the contrasts are correlated and the noise-free ones show no matching gain). Even where the cap binds in no log (1,000 people, one draw) five of six noisy means are negative (−0.001 to −0.004) and both noise-free contrasts are negative with upper bounds near +0.0001: one draw changes who is drawn, not how many. **Where it binds it costs more:** ratebeer (one draw: 78% of the 100 people per round are left, 28% of the 300; five draws: 70% of 300; ten: 94%), beeradvocate (one draw, 300 people: 47%) and ml-latest (one draw, 300 people: 91%). At 300 people with one draw all six noisy means are negative (−0.002 to −0.016, lower bounds down to −0.049) and the noise-free Gaussian contrast is −0.019 [−0.052, +0.002].

3. **Where the pool does not run out, one draw at the full budget beats several draws at a fraction of it, for both noises, even though the single draw has the smaller lifetime (8 against 10–10.4).** One draw at ε 8 loses 49% / 24% / 5% (Gaussian) and 17% / 8% / 3% (Laplace) at 100 / 300 / 1,000 people, while five draws at ε 4 (Gaussian, lifetime 10.4) lose 65% / 42% / 13% and at ε 2 (Laplace, lifetime 10) 63% / 35% / 9%. The five- and ten-draw rows come from logs where a cap of five or ten does not bind; they matter for communities whose members come back (ratebeer-like logs, not reported separately), not as a general design.
4. **In people, the Laplace advantage shrinks from about 3× to about 1.2–1.5× to about 1.0–1.25×.** Gaussian people needed for the same lost share as the Laplace arm (interpolated; read at 100 and 300 people; computed on the 17 logs present at every size, which do not include ratebeer, beeradvocate or ml-latest, the only logs where the cap bites): per round and with single use at ε = 4, 3.1× and 3.0× (ε = 2: 2.8× and 2.4–2.5×; ε = 8: 3.7× and 3.2–3.4×); five draws, lifetime about 10: 1.2× and 1.2×, about 20: 1.4× and 1.5×; ten draws, about 16: 1.0× and 1.0×, about 35: 1.2× and 1.2×. **The "about 3 times the people" of doc 21's plain reading is a per-round figure and holds under a lifetime guarantee only if nobody is drawn twice.** (Added after a partial look; section 1, point 4.)
5. **A larger L1 bound loses.** At ε = 4 per round, bound C = 2 loses 0.088, 0.088, 0.020 against C = 1 (100, 300, 1,000 people) and C = 4 loses 0.167, 0.214, 0.095, although the noise-free signal improves by +0.019, +0.014, +0.009 and +0.028, +0.021, +0.013: noise grows with C faster than the signal. C = 1 stays.
6. **Every lifetime figure rests on a neighbouring relation, and on a public catalogue and a public list of who is active, that this simulation does not test:** a drawn person's report is replaced by an empty one (section 4, point 2). Under a swap relation the Laplace noise doubles and the Gaussian noise grows by √2, which cuts the one-round noise ratio at ε = 4 from 3.06 to 2.16 (standard deviation) and from 1.62 to 1.15 (expected maximum). Existing arms bracket the effect for single use at swap-ε = 4: the Laplace arm is the existing ε = 2 arm (noise scale 2/4), the Gaussian needs σ = √2·1.081 = 1.53, between the existing ε = 4 (σ 1.081) and ε = 2 (σ 1.994) arms, so the Laplace gain lies between +0.025 and +0.098 at 100 people, +0.052 and +0.178 at 300 and +0.024 and +0.148 at 1,000, against +0.156, +0.187, +0.064 under the stated relation. Laplace stays ahead at every size (the lower ends are positive; differences of means, no intervals); the gain falls at 100 people, the bracket barely constrains it at 300 and does not at 1,000, where it may grow (inference from existing arms, assuming the selector value falls as σ grows).
7. **For a registered test:** the single-use design carries the clean claim (lifetime ε equals per-round ε, the largest gains, no composition); it was chosen after a partial look at w1, so its confirmation on w3 is the real test. Five draws is the second contrast; ten draws is mixed and is not proposed (section 5).

## 1. How this came about, and the design

1. **Question.** Doc 21 compared Laplace and Gaussian noise at the same ε per round and said the per-round ε is not the same privacy for a person drawn many times (pure ε adds up linearly over releases, the Gaussian only like √k). Its plain-language reading ("about 3 times the people") was per round. Leo asked whether new runs were needed. Doc 21 section 5 listed what had no development number: the lifetime-matched cells at larger k, a participation cap, and the L1 bound C = 2 and 4. This is that run.
2. **Code (new files; the engine and earlier programmes are unchanged).** `scripts/ssp/cap_core.py` adds one optional scheme parameter `kmax=K` to `gaussn` (L2 unit length, analytic Gaussian) and `lapn` (L1 unit, Laplace). One sampler instance is one draw's 156 rounds. In a round the eligible people are those active in it who were drawn fewer than K times so far; the round draws n of them (all if fewer) with the std sampler's stream, so until someone reaches the cap the draw is the plain draw, and every arm with the same K draws the same people whatever its noise. A round with nobody eligible releases nothing. `scripts/ssp/check_cap.py` checks it; `scripts/ssp/tune_cap.py` builds cells, runs them and reports.
3. **Cells.** The same 68 cells as programme L, built from the stored weekly cells (same logs, same plain arms, same seeds; the plain arms reproduce the stored cell in 68 of 68 cells of both row files). Each cell was run twice, once per set, adding the days arms under 16 samplers (main set) and under 8 (single-use set). **Main set (16):** five draws: Gaussian ε 4 and 8, Laplace ε 2 and 4; ten draws: Gaussian ε 4 and 8, Laplace ε 1.6 and 3.5; noise-free references of both kinds at each K; no cap, bound C = 2 and 4 at ε 4 with their noise-free references. **Single-use set (8):** one draw, Gaussian and Laplace at the same ε 2, 4, 8, and noise-free references. The Laplace ε of each pair is the lifetime ε of the Gaussian pair divided by K, rounded **down** to the grid (exact: 2.084, 4.433, 1.614, 3.562; used: 2, 4, 1.6, 3.5), so Laplace is slightly better protected than the Gaussian arm. Lifetime ε: Gaussian by exact composition (σ/√K, δ = 10⁻⁵, `laplace_dev_lifetime.csv`), Laplace K·ε with δ = 0.
4. **Order of the work (what was planned and what was not).** The K = 5 and 10 pairs and the L1 bound were written down before the first run. The single-use set was added after a partial look at the main set (33 of 68 cells, K = 5 and 10: Laplace ahead at five draws, mixed at ten), because doc 21's arithmetic said the advantage fades with K and one draw is the case without composition. The people-equivalent table (section 2, point 4) was added to the report after the main set had finished. Nothing else changed after the first look (the ε grid, the contrasts and the sampler code were fixed before the run; only the report code and docstrings were extended). The single-use contrasts and the people-equivalents are therefore post hoc, and the single-use design is also the main proposal in section 5. The main set took 98 minutes (4 workers, quiet scheduler), the single-use set 27.
5. **Measure.** As in doc 21: selector value of the days arms under each sampler (the stored plain arm minus it is the price), lost share = price ÷ plain selector value, contrasts paired over logs with a 95% bootstrap interval over logs (seed 7).

### Interpretation

1. **Under the stated relation and a public catalogue, single use keeps the per-round advantage.** With nobody drawn twice the lifetime ε equals the per-round ε, so no composition argument is needed, and the Laplace arm is ahead at the same ε in 22–26 of 24–27 logs at 100 and 300 people and in 14–16 of 17 at 1,000; all nine intervals exclude 0 and the people-equivalent is 2.5–3.7. The single-use arms cost little on average against uncapped sampling (Gaussian ε = 4: 65% lost against 66% at 100 people; 44% against 42% at 300; 13% against 13% at 1,000; Laplace ε = 4: 39% against 41%, 16% against 15%, 4% against 4%); at 300 people every difference goes the same way (more lost with the cap), as section 2, point 2 shows.
2. **Single use needs a pool that does not run out, and it is costly where people come back.** In 26 of 27, 21 of 24 and 17 of 17 logs the people per round stay at 0.99 of plain or above; the exceptions are communities where few people send most rows. In doc 21's exposure table, with uncapped draws over 52 weeks, 54% and 64% of the people drawn in ratebeer are drawn twice or more at 100 and 300 people per round (beeradvocate 41% and 53%, ml-latest 17% and 26%), against a median of 1.7% and 4.5%. The people-equivalents exclude these logs, and 20 of the 27 logs are Amazon logs where people rarely return; a deployment whose users come back often looks more like ratebeer.
3. **With more draws per person the gain shrinks, in the direction of doc 21's arithmetic.** Pure ε adds up (K·ε), the Gaussian less than linearly, so the Laplace ε of an equal-lifetime pair falls like 1/K. At ε = 4 the one-round standard-deviation ratio (Gaussian ÷ Laplace) is 3.06 and falls to 1.59 at five draws and 1.23 at ten, and the ratio of expected maxima (which matter for the top of the list) from 1.62 to 0.84 and 0.65; doc 21's arithmetic bracketed the crossover only loosely (K = 4 for the expected maximum, K = 19 for the standard deviation) and the simulation falls inside that bracket. Five draws still favour Laplace in six of six contrasts (four intervals exclude 0; the two that include 0 are lifetime about 10 at 100 people and about 20 at 1,000 people). Ten draws: the Gaussian is ahead at lifetime about 16 with 100 people (−0.019, interval just excludes 0, Laplace ahead in 13 of 27 logs; level at 300 and 1,000 people) and the Laplace arm is ahead at about 35 (+0.030, +0.027 at 100 and 300 people; level at 1,000).
4. **The size of the advantage depends on the lifetime level and on the size.** At 1,000 people the lost shares are already small (4–13%), so five and ten draws leave a gain of at most +0.026 (and +0.007 or less for lifetime about 20 and above).
5. **A cap is cheap where people rarely return and costs something where they do.** Its cost is small and mostly negative even where it binds in no log (1,000 people, one draw: −0.001 to −0.004, because one draw changes who is drawn); where it binds (three logs at 300 people with one draw) the means are consistently negative. The six intervals above 0 (mostly at 100 people with one draw) are not explained by signal: the noise-free contrasts there show no gain (Gaussian −0.008, Laplace +0.0002), and six of 36 is more than the 0.9 expected above 0 by chance, but the contrasts are correlated; treat them as unexplained, not as a benefit.
6. **The L1 bound C = 1 is the best of the three tried at ε = 4**: a bound above 1 pays the noise on every item and gains less signal than it costs. C = 2 with Laplace noise is still ahead of the Gaussian at C = 1 (+0.047 to +0.096). The tuning gap does not flatter Laplace: every drawn person has at least one row, so both norms are at least 1 and a bound below 1 only rescales signal and noise together; C = 1 is the smallest bound that matters.
7. **One draw at the full budget beats five draws at a fraction of it** (section 0, point 3; lost shares from `cap_dev_grid.csv`), even though the single draw has the smaller lifetime: lifetime 8, one draw: Gaussian ε 8 49% / 24% / 5%, Laplace ε 8 17% / 8% / 3%; lifetime about 10, five draws: Gaussian ε 4 65% / 42% / 13%, Laplace ε 2 63% / 35% / 9%. Where the pool allows it, the larger lever is fewer draws; the noise choice still matters under single use (49% → 17% at 100 people).

## 4. Limits and disclosures

1. **Development only.** Window w1 is the development window (it is also where doc 14's selector was designed); none of the new samplers is in a registry. The single-use set and the people-equivalents are post hoc (section 1, point 4). Thresholds of a registered test are still to be written (section 5).
2. **The neighbouring relation, the catalogue and the trusted server.** The lifetime figures hold for this relation: a drawn person's report is replaced by an empty one (add/remove of the person's data, sensitivity C for both noises), while who is active in a round and who is drawn are public, like the catalogue (items already seen by anyone, so every release depends on everyone's earlier rows). They are not covered if the person's rows are swapped for other rows (L1 sensitivity 2C, L2 sensitivity √2·C; bracketed in section 0, point 6, not simulated), nor if who is active is itself private (removing a person changes who else is drawn and whether a round releases anything: an Opus reviewer built a small example where one person is released with person 8 present and nobody without). Central model with a trusted server: with secure aggregation and no trusted server the noise has to be produced in a distributed way (Bagdasaryan et al. 2022 use a distributed discrete Laplace for pure DP, doc 90, section 6, item 5); not simulated. Nominal accounting, no amplification by sampling, not a certified guarantee.
3. **The cap counts all 156 rounds (three years) of a cell**, warm-up included. A system that runs longer exhausts any fixed lifetime cap; three logs show it already within three years.
4. **Gaussian δ = 10⁻⁵, Laplace δ = 0.** At equal ε that favours Laplace per round (δ = 0 is the stronger guarantee) and the exact lifetime composition favours the Gaussian over many releases (doc 21, section 3). Charging Laplace K·ε is not unfair at these K: an Opus reviewer's tight composition of the Laplace arms at δ = 10⁻⁵ gives 10.000, 20.000, 15.990 and 34.990 for the four pairs, essentially K·ε.
5. **The people-equivalents are interpolations** between three sizes (they use the 17 logs present at every size, which exclude ratebeer, beeradvocate and ml-latest), with two extrapolated values. They translate the lost-share differences into people; they are not a separate measurement and say nothing about the logs where single use is costly.
6. **Statistics.** Intervals over logs treat logs as independent (20 of 27 are Amazon); one seed and 8 draws per cell; the three sizes use different log sets (27, 24 and 17 logs); contrasts at the three sizes are not independent of each other. The per-log people shares behind the three exceptions are in `cap_dev_people.csv`; per-log selector values are not tabulated.
7. **Not run:** caps of 2–4, 20 and 52 draws, 3,000 people, rounds other than 7 days, windows other than w1, the swap relation, the Laplace bound C other than at ε = 4 and without a cap, and a floating-point-safe Laplace sampler (doc 21, limit 3).
8. **Reviews are same-provider, not an outside audit** (section 6).

## 6. Checks and review
